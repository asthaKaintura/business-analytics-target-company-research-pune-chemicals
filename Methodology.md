1. Objective
The objective of this study was to identify 25 SME-scale specialty chemical manufacturers with genuine operational presence in Pune, India, and evaluate them using a structured multi-criteria scoring framework.

2. Scope and Selection Criteria
The study focused only on specialty chemical manufacturers. Companies involved in trading, distribution, pure services, or large conglomerates were excluded. Only firms with operational manufacturing presence in Pune or surrounding industrial zones (MIDC areas such as Chakan, Bhosari, and Talegaon) were considered.

3. Data Collection Process
The initial list of companies was gathered through manual online research using Google search, company websites, their linkdln profiles and publicly available industrial listings. Keywords such as “specialty chemicals Pune,” “chemical manufacturers MIDC Pune,” and “specialty chemical pvt ltd SMEs pune based ” were used to identify relevant firms.
Each company was individually verified using its official website (where available), company descriptions, and product portfolios to confirm manufacturing activity and sector classification.

4. Filtering Approach
A multi-stage filtering process was applied:

    Step 1: Manufacturer verification
    Companies were screened to ensure they had in-house manufacturing capabilities. Trading firms, distributors, and service-based companies were removed.

    Step 2: Sector filtering
    Only specialty chemical companies were retained. Commodity chemical producers and unrelated industries were excluded.

    Step 3: Geographic validation
    Companies were included only if they had operational presence (manufacturing plant or R&D unit) in Pune or nearby industrial regions.

    Step 4: Financial constraint
    Companies exceeding ₹500 Cr revenue were excluded to maintain SME focus.

    Step 5: Activity check
    Companies with outdated or inactive business presence were removed.

5. Scoring Framework (Federer Score Model)
Each company was evaluated using a six-parameter scoring system:

     C1: Manufacturing capability
     C2: India-based operational presence
     C3: Product differentiation and technical depth
     C4: Technical strength of leadership
     C5: Sector growth potential
     C6: Observable growth signals

6. Yield Expectation and Data Quality
During the research process, it was observed that a significant portion of identified companies did not meet the required criteria. Approximately 70% of companies were filtered out due to issues such as incorrect sector classification, lack of manufacturing presence, or scale mismatch. This resulted in an expected yield of around 30%, aligning with real-world industrial data variability.

7. Tools and Sources Used
The dataset was built using:

Manual Google search queries
Official company websites
Public industrial directories and listings
Cross-verification of company profiles and product pages by visiting their linkdln profiles

No automated scraping tools were used. The analysis was conducted manually to ensure accuracy and contextual understanding of each company.

8. Key Learnings
The study highlighted that the chemical manufacturing sector in India is highly fragmented, with many SMEs operating in niche segments. It also demonstrated that company classification requires careful verification, as many firms appear similar at surface level but differ significantly in manufacturing capability and scale.
